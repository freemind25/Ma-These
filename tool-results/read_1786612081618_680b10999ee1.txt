    17→    17→Over many years, I have guided students in their thesis work. As a
    18→    18→supervisor for over 10 PhD candidates and 100 bachelor’s and master’s theses, and as a reviewer or examiner for more than 1000 theses, I have witnessed the challenges the students have faced. I have
    19→    19→seen them wrestling with the formulations of their research questions,
    20→    20→struggling with their literature reviews, sweating over their data collection and data analysis, and grappling with their writing. Yet, I
    21→    21→have also seen all this hard work bearing fruit in well-crafted theses
    22→    22→with original, significant findings, making them the pinnacle of the
    23→    23→students’ academic work. Generative AI is promising, or threatening,
    24→    24→to change all of this.
    25→    25→Generative AI tools, such as ChatGPT, will be able to support all
    26→    26→parts and aspects of thesis writing. Students will have access to multitudes of virtual, but devoted and indefatigable, experts, research
    27→    27→assistants, supervisors, mentors, and editors. Will these tools take out
    28→    28→the drudgery in thesis writing and leave the fun and creative parts?
    29→    29→Will they enable students to produce theses with higher quality, and
    30→    30→learn more in the process, than ever before? Or will they lure the students into soulless and automated writing with predictable and dull
    31→    31→results? And what will the role of human supervisors be in this new
    32→    32→landscape?
    33→    33→This book does not answer these questions. Instead, it takes a more
    34→    34→practical approach by providing instruments, in the form of guidelines and prompts, that students and supervisors can use to act on
    35→    35→these questions — the book is rather about changing than explaining.
    36→    36→The instruments encourage students to work with generative AI in an
    37→    37→iterative, open-ended, creative and critical way instead of just making
    38→    38→the writing more efficient. While generative AI is here to stay as a vital part of the thesis writing process, it is up to students and teachers
    39→    39→to form its use.

    45→    45→proved a valuable resource for making up for my limited knowledge

   295→   295→A thesis is the culminating work of your degree, be it a bachelor, a
   296→   296→master or a PhD. It will demonstrate your expertise and interest in a
   297→   297→particular subject within your field of study. A thesis is also a valuable
   298→   298→learning experience in itself that will prepare you for academic work,

   302→   302→the purpose of writing a thesis
   303→   303→
   304→   304→Writing a thesis serves a practical purpose, as it is often a requirement
   305→   305→for obtaining a degree. However, there are additional and deeper benefits that go beyond this. By writing a thesis, you develop skills in
   306→   306→generating, exploring, synthesizing, and communicating knowledge
   307→   307→and ideas.
   308→   308→Creating Knowledge Through Research. Writing a thesis will teach
   309→   309→you how to conduct systematic investigations to discover new knowledge or affirm known truths. It provides hands-on experience in designing and implementing research methods, such as experiments,
   310→   310→case studies, interviews, and data analysis. You will gain proficiency
   311→   311→in managing projects, setting goals, achieving results, and meeting
   312→   312→deadlines. You will learn about attention to detail, technical skills,
   313→   313→problem-solving as well as patience and persistence. In other words,
   314→   314→by writing a thesis, you will learn how to become a researcher.
   315→   315→Exploring and Synthesizing Through Scholarship. By writing a
   316→   316→thesis, you will learn to delve deeply into a topic, which is usually
   317→   317→not possible during ordinary coursework. You will experience how to
   318→   318→learn from different sources, in particular academic articles, to gather
   319→   319→

   321→   321→

   325→   325→
   326→   326→diverse ideas and perspectives. You will come to see the relationships
   327→   327→between your thesis and existing knowledge in the field. You will
   328→   328→learn to discuss and defend your work, both with your supervisor
   329→   329→and fellow students. You will come to understand how critical thinking, curiosity, open-mindedness, and humility can help to enhance
   330→   330→your thesis. In other words, by writing a thesis, you will learn how to
   331→   331→become a scholar.
   332→   332→Communicating Through Academic Writing. By writing a thesis,
   333→   333→you will learn how to communicate with an audience through the
   334→   334→written word. You will acquire the skill to express complex thoughts
   335→   335→with clarity and precision. You will learn to write coherently and concisely, ensuring that your text flows logically without being verbose.
   336→   336→And you will learn how to structure a long academic text in a readerfriendly manner. In other words, by writing a thesis, you will learn
   337→   337→how to become an academic writer.
   338→   338→An analogy can help to clarify the differences between a scholar, a researcher and an academic writer. A scholar is like someone who has a
   339→   339→vast collection of LEGO sets. They have studied each set’s instructions
   340→   340→closely and can explain how various pieces and sets connect. They appreciate the designs others have made and can tell you all about them.
   341→   341→They love to learn, discuss, and share their knowledge about these
   342→   342→LEGO sets. In contrast, a researcher is like a LEGO enthusiast who
   343→   343→doesn’t just stick to the instructions. Instead, they experiment with
   344→   344→the bricks, trying to create something new. They love to innovate and
   345→   345→discover new possibilities with the bricks. And an academic writer
   346→   346→is like the person who writes and illustrates the LEGO instruction
   347→   347→manuals. They take the knowledge from the LEGO collector (scholar)
   348→   348→and the innovations from the LEGO experimenter (researcher) and
   349→   349→create clear, step-by-step guides for others to follow. They are skilled
   350→   350→in presenting information in a clear, organized, and understandable
   351→   351→manner.
   352→   352→In the academic world, the roles of researcher, scholar, and academic writer are usually performed by the same individual. While
   353→   353→each role has its distinct characteristics, they should not be separated,
   354→   354→as the activities of inquiry, discovery, and dissemination contribute to
   355→   355→and enhance each other.

   358→   358→the imrad structure
   359→   359→
   360→   360→Research findings can be presented in different ways, but using a standardized structure makes it easier for readers to follow the logic and
   361→   361→reasoning of the presentation. The IMRAD (Introduction, Method, Results And Discussion) structure has become an established format for
   362→   362→organising both research papers and theses in the natural as well as
   363→   363→social sciences. IMRAD suggests a division into four parts:
   364→   364→• Introduction — Why did you start the study? This section aims to
   365→   365→provide an overview of the research presented in the thesis. It
   366→   366→
   367→   367→1.3 generative ai: boon or bane?
   368→   368→
   369→   369→5
   370→   370→
   371→   371→begins by providing a brief introduction to the area of study.
   372→   372→It then goes on to explain the research problem and formulate
   373→   373→research questions or goals. This section also provides a conceptual framework, explaining and discussing the key notions of
   374→   374→the thesis. Furthermore, it includes a literature review, summarizing existing research including current knowledge gaps.
   375→   375→• Method — What did you do and how? This section describes the
   376→   376→research strategies, methods of data collection, and data analysis that were employed, and explains how they were utilized. It
   377→   377→provides a rationale for the choice of these strategies and methods, and also addresses research ethics.
   378→   378→• Results — What did you find? This section answers the research
   379→   379→question of the thesis. It presents and explains the research results, thereby often including tables, graphs, charts, and figures
   380→   380→to represent the results visually.
   381→   381→• Discussion — What does it mean? This section presents a critical
   382→   382→analysis and interpretation of the research conducted and its
   383→   383→results. It highlights limitations that were encountered during
   384→   384→the study, compares the results to those of prior research, and
   385→   385→discusses the practical and theoretical implications of the outcomes. Furthermore, it outlines future research areas that could
   386→   386→be explored.

   481→   481→Forms of Prompts
   482→   482→Prompts come in many forms and shapes, and some of the most common ones are listed below. These forms of prompts can be combined
   483→   483→in order to craft a prompt that conveys your intention and helps ChatGPT provide a focused and relevant answer.
   484→   484→Standard Prompts. Standard prompts are a simple way to guide
   485→   485→ChatGPT by providing a specific task for it to complete. Standard
   486→   486→prompts usually start with a word, such as “Write”, “Create” or “Gen-
   487→   487→
   488→   488→1.4 prompt engineering for thesis writing
   489→   489→
   490→   490→erate”, followed by a noun phrase. Standard prompts can be seen as
   491→   491→the basic form of prompts, which can be extended in various ways.
   492→   492→Some examples are:
   493→   493→• Write a review of this article
   494→   494→• Create themes for these responses
   495→   495→• Explain the process of photosynthesis
   496→   496→Instruction Prompts. Instruction prompts extend standard prompts
   497→   497→by providing explicit instructions on how ChatGPT should perform a
   498→   498→task. Providing detailed instructions allows ChatGPT to give relevant
   499→   499→answers that reflect your intentions. Some examples are:
   500→   500→• Write a review of this movie that focuses on the protagonist
   501→   501→• Create interview questions that are friendly and easy to understand
   502→   502→• Explain the process of photosynthesis, detailing the key stages,
   503→   503→the role of chlorophyll, and the importance of sunlight and carbon dioxide
   504→   504→Role Prompts. Role Prompts are prompts in which you ask ChatGPT
   505→   505→to take on a certain role, e.g a researcher, a student or a lawyer. Some
   506→   506→examples are:
   507→   507→• As a researcher, explain the process of photosynthesis
   508→   508→• As a journalist, explain the process of photosynthesis
   509→   509→• As a lawyer, explain GDPR
   510→   510→Audience Prompts. Audience prompts are prompts in which you ask
   511→   511→ChatGPT to provide answers that are suitable for a specific audience,
   512→   512→e.g. children, experts, or non-experts. Some examples are:
