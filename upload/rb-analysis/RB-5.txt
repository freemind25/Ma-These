KEY CONCEPTS AND FRAMEWORKS

  SEVEN ASPECTS OF COMPARISON:

  ASPECT 1 — DEFINITION
    Theoretical:  Broad set of ideas and principles offering a general
                  perspective on a topic.
    Conceptual:  Specific concepts and ideas that the researcher applies
                  directly within the study context.

  ASPECT 2 — BASIS
    Theoretical:  Grounded in established theories from literature, tested
                  and validated by other scholars.
    Conceptual:  Built on key concepts representing main variables and
                  their relationships in the study.

  ASPECT 3 — NATURE
    Theoretical:  Conceptual model that guides the study, its assumptions,
                  and interpretation of results.
    Conceptual:  Researcher-developed model explaining how concepts interact
                  and influence one another. May adapt existing theory.

  ASPECT 4 — DEVELOPMENT & ACCEPTANCE
    Theoretical:  Well-established, widely recognized, accepted within the
                  academic community.
    Conceptual:  Developed by researcher specifically for the study, proposed
                  to address the research problem.

  ASPECT 5 — PURPOSE
    Theoretical:  Provides broad perspective to understand and position
                  research within existing body of knowledge.
    Conceptual:  Shows logical flow of research inquiry and how key concepts
                  will be examined.

  ASPECT 6 — COMPONENTS
    Theoretical:  Interrelated theories, constructs, and propositions
                  derived from prior research.
    Conceptual:  Core concepts and their connections illustrating the
                  relationships to be studied.

  ASPECT 7 — USE
    Theoretical:  Test theories, make predictions, guide research within
                  wider context.
    Conceptual:  Build or refine theory, provide practical insight relevant
                  to specific research context.

  FOOTER KEY TAKEAWAYS:
    "STRONG RESEARCH STARTS WITH THE RIGHT FRAMEWORK."
    ✔ Use theory to understand the big picture.
    ✔ Use concepts to answer your research question.

---

CLASSIFICATION SYSTEM

  DEDUCTIVE vs INDUCTIVE PARADIGM:
    Theoretical Framework → Deductive (testing existing knowledge)
    Conceptual Framework → Inductive/Adaptive (building new knowledge)

  SCOPE SPECTRUM:
    Theoretical: Broad, general, community-validated, theory-based
    Conceptual: Specific, context-bound, researcher-created, variable-based

---

DERIVED RULES FOR AI PROMPTS

  Rule 1 — SCOPE RULE
    If goal = general background / big picture → prioritize Theoretical
    Framework.
    If goal = define specific variables and interactions → prioritize
    Conceptual Framework.

  Rule 2 — ORIGIN RULE
    If user needs pre-existing, cited academic theories → Theoretical.
    If user needs to synthesize own variable definitions → Conceptual.

  Rule 3 — DIRECTIONALITY RULE
    Deductive reasoning (testing hypothesis against established truth) →
    Theoretical Framework.
    Inductive reasoning (developing new model from observations) →
    Conceptual Framework.

  Rule 4 — COMPONENT IDENTIFICATION RULE
    Theoretical: Look for constructs, propositions, validated models
    (e.g., Maslow's Hierarchy).
    Conceptual: Look for operationalized variables, flow diagrams,
    defined relationships specific to user's data.

  Rule 5 — NEW vs OLD RULE
    Novel/exploratory research area → Default to Conceptual Framework.
    Mature/well-trodden research area → Default to Theoretical Framework.

  EXAMPLE APPLICATION:
    "I want to analyze employee motivation using Maslow's theory."
    → Theoretical Framework approach (established theory)

    "I need to map out how remote work affects my specific team's
    productivity based on my survey data."
    → Conceptual Framework approach (building a specific model)
